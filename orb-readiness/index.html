Technical capability alone does not establish qualification.

01 / the question

Can a firm turn capability into accepted evidence?

Our Right to Build (ORB) proposes studying documentation and qualification burden in aerospace and advanced-manufacturing supply chains. Its working hypothesis is that evidence requirements can constrain participation even when technical capability exists.

I am developing the requirements, proposed study design and source-verification framework. The hypothesis is not an empirical finding, and scholarly novelty has not been established.

Current research stage

  • Requirements gathering only
  • No fieldwork, recruited sample or participant dataset
  • No validated scoring instrument
  • No entity, funding or site control
  • No authority to certify or qualify firms

02 / try the concept

Readiness belongs to an opportunity.

A firm may hold the same evidence while different buyers ask different questions. Select a fictional opportunity to explore the proposed firm-opportunity unit. These invented requirements are teaching examples, not actual procurement requirements or an ORB assessment.

04 / source discipline

Source authority and claim status are different.

The evidence register distinguishes verified, partially verified, not located and not established claims. A search that does not locate a record supports a dated search result, not a claim of nonexistence or misconduct.

Some contextual findings rely on office correspondence retained in restricted research records. This portfolio review did not inspect or reproduce those originals. They are separate from supplier-study outcomes, of which ORB has none.
